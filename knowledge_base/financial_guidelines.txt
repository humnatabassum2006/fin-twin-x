FINANCIAL HEALTH GUIDELINES

1. SAVINGS
A healthy savings habit helps improve financial stability.
A commonly used target is to save around 20% of monthly income when possible.
If the savings rate is low, the person should gradually increase savings and reduce unnecessary expenses.

2. EMERGENCY FUND
An emergency fund provides financial protection against unexpected expenses or income loss.
A useful target is to maintain savings that can cover at least 3 to 6 months of essential expenses.
People with less than 3 months of emergency savings should prioritize building their emergency fund.

3. DEBT
Debt-to-income ratio helps measure how much of a person's income is committed to debt.
A lower debt-to-income ratio generally indicates better debt management.
If debt becomes too high compared with income, the person should focus on reducing debt and avoiding unnecessary borrowing.

4. FINANCIAL HEALTH
Financial health depends on multiple factors including savings, debt management, and emergency savings.
A strong financial position usually includes a healthy savings rate, manageable debt, and sufficient emergency savings.

5. FINANCIAL STRESS
Income reduction can negatively affect financial health.
An increase in expenses can reduce savings and weaken financial stability.
A combined decrease in income and increase in expenses can create significant financial stress.

6. FINANCIAL RECOMMENDATIONS
If savings are low, increase savings gradually and review unnecessary spending.
If debt is high, prioritize debt reduction.
If emergency savings are low, build an emergency fund before taking unnecessary financial risks.
Financial decisions should consider the person's income, expenses, savings, debt, and financial goals.

7. RISK MANAGEMENT
People should maintain an emergency fund and avoid taking financial risks they cannot afford.
Diversification and careful financial planning can help manage investment risk.
Financial recommendations should be based on the individual's financial situation rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.
